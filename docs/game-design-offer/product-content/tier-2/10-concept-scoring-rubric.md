# Worksheet 10: Concept Scoring Rubric
### Grade your concept against professional design criteria


> **Design question:** How does this concept compare to a well-designed game concept?
>
> **Time to complete:** 15–20 minutes
>
> **When to use:** After completing the Viability Scorecard (Worksheet 6). This is the extended version — it uses weighted dimensions and sub-criteria for a more nuanced assessment.
>
> **What you'll have when you're done:** A weighted score out of 112.5 with a letter grade (A/B/C/F) and your three weakest sub-criteria named.

---

## How This Differs from the Viability Scorecard

The Viability Scorecard (Worksheet 6) gives you a fast binary verdict. This rubric gives you a detailed breakdown you can use to:
- Identify exactly which sub-criteria are holding your score down
- Compare your concept against a reference game you admire
- Get a letter grade with specific improvement guidance

---

## Scoring Instructions

Each dimension has 2–3 sub-criteria. Score each sub-criterion 1–5.

**Weight multipliers** reflect which dimensions matter most:
- Core mechanics and loops (weighted 1.5×) — if these are weak, nothing else saves the concept
- Scope and risk (weighted 1.0×) — important but can be resolved through planning
- Motivation (weighted 0.75×) — real, but less objectively measurable

## Dimension A: Core Mechanic (Weight: 1.5×)

**Maximum: 15 points × 1.5 = 22.5 points**

```
A1. Core verb clarity
    (Is the single core action identifiable and specific?)
    1 = No core verb defined
    3 = Core verb exists but is vague or compound
    5 = One clear verb, specific and interesting
    Score: ___ / 5

A2. Input→output clarity
    (Can someone understand what the player does and what happens from 2 sentences?)
    1 = Unclear even to me
    3 = I understand it but can't explain it simply
    5 = Explainable in one sentence to someone unfamiliar with the game
    Score: ___ / 5

A3. Feel specificity
    (Can you describe what success feels like without using "satisfying" or "fun"?)
    1 = No idea what it should feel like
    3 = General direction but not specific
    5 = Specific, evocative description that could guide prototype feedback
    Score: ___ / 5
```

## Dimension B: Loop Completeness (Weight: 1.5×)

**Maximum: 15 points × 1.5 = 22.5 points**

```
B1. Moment-to-moment loop
    (Is the atomic loop — action → feedback → micro-reward — fully defined?)
    1 = No moment-to-moment loop defined
    3 = Loop exists but feedback or micro-reward is vague
    5 = All three components defined, loop takes under 30 seconds
    Score: ___ / 5

B2. Session loop
    (Does the session have a goal, tension, and resolution?)
    1 = No session structure defined
    3 = Goal exists, tension/resolution are vague
    5 = All three defined, session is 5–30 minutes with clear endpoint
    Score: ___ / 5

B3. Long-term loop
    (Does the player have a reason to return after a session ends?)
    1 = No retention hook
    3 = Some progression but unclear why players return
    5 = Clear progression system, retention hook, and meta-goal
    Score: ___ / 5

Subtotal (before weight): ___ / 15
Weighted score (× 1.5):   ___ / 22.5

Comparative note:
_______________________________________________________________
```

## Dimension D: Scope Realism (Weight: 1.0×)

**Maximum: 15 points × 1.0 = 15 points**

```
D1. Constraint accuracy
    (Have you named your real constraints — not optimistic estimates?)
    1 = No constraints defined
    3 = Constraints listed but may be optimistic
    5 = Honest constraints with a plan for what gets cut under pressure
    Score: ___ / 5

D2. Feature-to-resource fit
    (Does every planned feature fit within your stated team / time / budget?)
    1 = Obvious mismatch between scope and resources
    3 = Close, but 2–3 features seem risky given constraints
    5 = Every feature is achievable within constraints, with buffer
    Score: ___ / 5

D3. Cut priority
    (Do you know what to cut first if you run out of time?)
    1 = No idea
    3 = Some features identified as optional but not prioritized
    5 = Clear cut order: must-have → should-have → nice-to-have
    Score: ___ / 5
```

## Dimension E: Differentiation (Weight: 1.0×)

**Maximum: 15 points × 1.0 = 15 points**

```

    (Do you know the 3 closest competing games and their strengths?)
    1 = No competitors identified
    3 = Competitors named, weaknesses not analyzed
    5 = 3 competitors named with specific what-you-do-differently for each
```
