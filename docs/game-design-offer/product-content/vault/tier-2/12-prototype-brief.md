> **Design question:** What should I build first, and how will I know if it works?
>
> **Time to complete:** 15 minutes
>
> **When to use:** After completing the Module Map (Worksheet 11) and Viability Scorecard (Worksheet 6). Use when you're ready to commit to building a prototype.

---

## Instructions

A prototype brief defines one hypothesis, one scope, and one success condition. If you write down three hypotheses, you have three prototypes — not one. Keep this document to a single focused test.

The goal is not to build "the game." The goal is to answer one question as cheaply as possible.

---

## 4. Success Criteria

> What does "this works" look like? Be specific — not "players like it" but a concrete, observable outcome.

## 5. Failure Criteria

> What does "this doesn't work" look like? Defining failure in advance prevents motivated reasoning during testing.

```
This prototype fails if:

Primary failure signal:
_____________________________________________________________
_____________________________________________________________

(What you'd observe in playtest that would make you say "this isn't working")

If the prototype fails, the next step is:
[ ] Revise the mechanic and test again
[ ] Revise the hypothesis — we're testing the wrong thing
[ ] Abandon this concept — the core assumption was wrong
[ ] Other: __________________________________________________
```

## 6. Timeline

---

## Completion Check

- [ ] The prototype goal is one hypothesis (not three)
- [ ] Success criteria are observable — I could measure them in a room watching someone play
- [ ] Failure criteria are defined before I start (not after I see disappointing results)
- [ ] My "what NOT to build" list is longer than my "what to build" list (if not, scope is probably too large)
- [ ] Timeline has a hard ceiling — I've decided in advance when I stop rather than expand
