## Problem Section

### Headline
> The concept phase is where most indie games silently fail.

### Body Copy

You have a game idea. Maybe a great one. But right now it lives in your head as a collection of vibes, references, and half-formed mechanics.

You can describe it to friends. You might have a mood board. Maybe a prototype that doesn't quite capture the vision yet.

But if someone asked you these questions, you'd hesitate:

- What is the core mechanic — the one interaction that must feel good for the game to work?
- What is the core loop — what does the player do repeatedly, and why do they keep doing it?
- Who exactly is this game for — and what are they comparing it to?
- What should be cut to keep scope realistic?
- Is this concept actually worth prototyping, or are you chasing a feeling?

Most indie games don't fail in production. They fail in concept — when the idea was too vague, the scope too ambitious, or the core too weak. The build just makes the failure expensive.

## How It Works Section

### Headline
> Five phases. One weekend. A clear concept.

### Process Steps

**1. Define the Seed**
What is this game? Why does it exist? What must it deliver? You start by forcing clarity on the fundamentals.

**2. Know Your Player**
Who is this for? What fantasy does it serve? What games is it compared to? You define the audience before designing for them.

**3. Isolate the Core**
What is the core mechanic? What is the core loop? What must feel good? You find the single thing that makes or breaks the game.

**4. Set the Constraints**
How big is this? What's the team, timeline, budget? What should be cut? You scope the concept to reality.

**5. Score the Verdict**
Is this worth prototyping? The Viability Scorecard gives you a structured, honest answer.

## FAQ Section

### Headline
> Questions.

**Do I need game design experience?**
No. The toolkit is designed for people who understand games but haven't formalized their design process. If you can describe why a game is fun, you can use this.

**What format is the product?**
PDF worksheets (designed, printable) plus editable markdown templates you can use in any text editor, Obsidian, or import into Notion.

**Is this a course?**
No. There are no lessons, no curriculum, no videos. It's a self-guided toolkit you apply directly to your own game concept.

**Will this work for my genre?**
Yes. The methodology is genre-agnostic. Core loops, mechanics, audience definition, and scope filtering apply to platformers, RPGs, shooters, puzzle games, simulations — any genre. The filled examples in Tier 2 demonstrate two different genres.

**What if I don't have a specific game idea yet?**
This product works best when you already have at least a rough idea. It structures and pressure-tests an existing concept — it doesn't generate ideas.

**Can't I find this information for free?**
You can find individual game design frameworks online. What you can't find is a structured, sequenced methodology that takes you from raw idea to scored, scoped, pitch-ready concept in one package. The value is in the structure, sequence, and pressure-testing — not the individual concepts.

**What if I'm not satisfied?**
14-day refund policy. If the toolkit doesn't help you structure your concept, request a refund.

**How do I get the product after purchase?**
Immediately. After checkout, you'll see a download page and receive a confirmation email with the same download link.

**Is this an AI tool?**
No. This is a human-designed methodology toolkit. No AI generation, no chatbots, no subscriptions.

**What's the difference between Tier 1 and Tier 2?**
Tier 1 gives you the core worksheets to test your concept. Tier 2 adds filled examples (so you can see a completed concept package), advanced frameworks (Colors of Game Design, Module Map), and output templates (Prototype Brief, Pitch Summary).
