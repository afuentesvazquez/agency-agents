# Risks & Objections

> Specialist: Credibility and Risk Auditor
> Status: DRAFT — pre-validation

---

## Purpose

This document attacks the offer from every angle a skeptical buyer, a competitor, or a bad review would. If a risk appears here, it must have a mitigation. If a mitigation is weak, it's flagged.

---

## Trust Risks

| Risk | Severity | Description | Mitigation | Mitigation Strength |
|------|----------|-------------|------------|-------------------|
| No track record | HIGH | Seller has no public game design portfolio or shipped titles | Lead with methodology credibility, not personal authority. Frame as "professional design frameworks adapted for indie use" — the frameworks are proven, even if the seller isn't famous. | MEDIUM — works at $19-39 but wouldn't work at $200+ |
| No testimonials at launch | HIGH | Zero social proof on day one | Use methodology proof instead: show worksheet previews, framework logic, and artifact quality. Add testimonials as they come in. Offer first buyers a discount for a review. | MEDIUM — acceptable for low-ticket, risky for longer |
| "Who are you to teach game design?" | HIGH | Audience may question credentials | Don't position as "teaching." Position as "providing structured tools." The product is a toolkit, not a course. You don't need credentials to sell a well-designed worksheet. | STRONG — reframing from teacher to toolmaker |
| Anonymous seller | MEDIUM | No visible founder identity | Add a brief, honest founder note on the landing page. Even one paragraph with a real name helps. | STRONG — easy fix |

## Audience Risks

| Risk | Severity | Description | Mitigation |
|------|----------|-------------|------------|
| Audience too broad | MEDIUM | "Indie game creators" ranges from hobbyists to professionals | Narrow primary messaging to "programmer with a game idea." Others can self-select in, but the copy targets one person. |
| Audience won't pay for worksheets | HIGH | Game devs are used to free resources (GDC talks, YouTube, free templates) | The value is in the structured methodology and sequence, not individual templates. Emphasize what free templates DON'T do: pressure-test viability, force hard decisions, create a complete package. |
| Audience is too small | LOW | There are millions of people with game ideas. The question is whether they're reachable. | Social content strategy targets existing communities (Reddit, Twitter, Discord). Audience size is not the risk — reachability is. |
| Audience doesn't self-identify | MEDIUM | People with vague game ideas may not realize they need "concept clarity" | Use problem-aware copy: "You have a game idea but don't know where to start" rather than "concept clarity toolkit." |

## Proof Risks

| Risk | Severity | Description | Mitigation |
|------|----------|-------------|------------|
| Can't show results | HIGH | No one has used the product yet to prove it works | Show the artifacts themselves as proof. Preview the worksheets. Show the filled examples. The quality of the tool IS the proof. |
| No "before/after" transformation | MEDIUM | Hard to show a dramatic outcome from a $19 toolkit | Frame the transformation as "from vague idea to structured concept" — show what the output looks like, not revenue or launch metrics. |
| Methodology isn't proprietary | MEDIUM | Core loop analysis, design pillars, etc. are established game design concepts | The value isn't inventing new theory — it's packaging existing methodology into a usable, sequenced toolkit. Acknowledge the sources. |

## Copy Risks

| Risk | Severity | Description | Mitigation |
|------|----------|-------------|------------|
| Sounds like a Notion template pack | HIGH | "Worksheets and templates" can sound commoditized | Emphasize the methodology, sequence, and design pressure-testing logic. Show the Viability Scorecard. Show the Colors of Game Design review. These are not generic templates. |
| Overpromises on outcomes | MEDIUM | "Pitch-ready concept pack" could set unrealistic expectations | Add clear limitations: "This product helps you structure your concept. It does not guarantee your game will be funded, greenlit, or successful." |
| Sounds too academic | MEDIUM | "Design lenses," "core mechanics," "module maps" can sound theoretical | Balance framework language with concrete outcomes: "Define your core loop" → "Know exactly what the player does every 30 seconds" |
| Condescending tone risk | HIGH | "Your idea isn't good enough" can alienate buyers | Frame as empowerment, not criticism: "Make your idea stronger" not "Fix your weak idea." The buyer is already motivated — validate that. |

## Pricing Risks

| Risk | Severity | Description | Mitigation |
|------|----------|-------------|------------|
| $19 feels too cheap for quality | LOW | Some buyers associate low price with low value | Visual quality of the product (PDF design, landing page) must signal premium craft. Price is low by design — it's a validation-stage product. |
| $39 doesn't feel like enough of a jump | MEDIUM | Buyer may not see why Tier 2 is worth 2x | The filled examples are the anchor. "See two complete concept packages" is a tangible value differentiator. |
| Competitors offer free alternatives | HIGH | Free GDD templates, free design frameworks exist online | Free resources are unstructured, unsequenced, and don't pressure-test. The product's value is in the methodology journey, not individual artifacts. |
| Race to the bottom | LOW | Someone could clone this at $9 | First-mover advantage matters less than quality and positioning. Focus on being good, not cheap. |

## Refund / Disappointment Risks

| Risk | Severity | Description | Mitigation |
|------|----------|-------------|------------|
| "I could have made this myself" | HIGH | Buyer realizes the templates aren't magic | Set expectations clearly on the landing page. The value is structure + sequence + pressure-testing, not secret knowledge. FAQ should address this directly. |
| "I didn't finish using it" | MEDIUM | Buyer downloads but never completes the worksheets | Not controllable. The product is a tool — usage is the buyer's responsibility. Focus on making the worksheets engaging and the workflow clear. |
| "It didn't match my genre/scope" | MEDIUM | Framework may feel too general for niche genres | The filled examples should demonstrate applicability across genres. Add a note: "These frameworks work for any genre — the examples show platformer and survival, but the methodology is universal." |
| High refund rate | LOW | At $19-39, most people won't bother requesting a refund even if disappointed | Offer a 14-day refund policy to reduce purchase anxiety. Low risk of abuse at this price point. |

## Fulfillment Risks

| Risk | Severity | Description | Mitigation |
|------|----------|-------------|------------|
| Delivery failure | LOW | Email doesn't arrive, download link breaks | Use Stripe success page as primary delivery (instant). Email as backup. Test the full flow before launch. |
| File format issues | LOW | PDFs don't render on certain devices | Use standard PDF format. Test on mobile, tablet, and desktop. |
| Product feels incomplete at launch | MEDIUM | Rushing to validate might result in thin content | Define minimum viable content clearly. Tier 1 needs 7 strong artifacts. Better to launch with 7 excellent worksheets than 15 mediocre ones. |

## Brand Risks

| Risk | Severity | Description | Mitigation |
|------|----------|-------------|------------|
| Looks like a cash grab | HIGH | Low-ticket info products have a reputation problem | Visual quality, honest copy, clear limitations, and methodology credibility fight this. No hype, no urgency manipulation, no fake scarcity. |
| Association with "guru" culture | HIGH | Game dev community is allergic to self-proclaimed experts selling courses | Never use guru language. Never claim to be an expert. Position as "I built a useful tool" not "I'll teach you." |
| Product name sounds generic | MEDIUM | "Concept Test" / "Concept Pack" aren't memorable | Good enough for v1. Brand naming is a post-validation problem. Clarity beats cleverness at launch. |
| Dark-pattern checkout perception | LOW | Aggressive CTAs, countdown timers, etc. | Don't use any dark patterns. No fake urgency, no countdown timers, no "only X left." Straightforward checkout. |

## Objection Handling (Pre-FAQ)

These objections WILL come up. Each needs a clear response ready for copy, FAQ, or DM conversations.

| Objection | Response Direction |
|-----------|-------------------|
| "I can find game design frameworks for free online" | Free resources are scattered, unsequenced, and don't pressure-test your specific concept. This product gives you a complete methodology in one package. |
| "Can't I just use ChatGPT to do this?" | ChatGPT generates text. This product provides structured methodology — the questions to ask, the sequence to follow, the scoring to apply. The framework is the value, not the fill-in. |
| "I don't need templates, I need to actually build" | If you build without concept clarity, you'll rebuild. This saves time downstream by catching design problems upstream. |
| "Who made this? What games have you shipped?" | This product packages established game design methodology into a usable toolkit. The frameworks are industry-standard. You're buying the structure, not the seller's resume. |
| "$19 is too expensive for worksheets" | It's less than a month of any subscription tool. If it saves you from 2 weeks of building the wrong thing, the ROI is 100x. |
| "Will this work for my specific genre?" | The methodology is genre-agnostic. Core loops, mechanics, audience definition, and scope filtering apply to every genre. The filled examples demonstrate this across two different genres. |
