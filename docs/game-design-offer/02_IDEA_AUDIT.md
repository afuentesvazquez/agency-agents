# Idea Audit

> Specialist: Senior Product Strategist
> Status: DRAFT — pre-validation

---

## Product Summary

**Product:** A low-ticket digital toolkit ($19/$39) that helps indie game creators convert vague game concepts into structured design artifacts — core mechanic definition, core loop mapping, audience/fantasy identification, scope filtering, and design risk assessment.

**Target buyer:** Solo indie creators, programmers with a game idea, aspiring design leads, small teams in concept phase.

**Delivery:** Digital, instant, self-guided.

---

## 10-Dimension Viability Audit

### 1. Clarity of Customer Pain

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **4 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | Real pain, but often undiagnosed. |

Most indie developers don't describe their problem as "I lack concept clarity." They describe it as "I keep starting projects and abandoning them," "I can't explain my game to anyone," or "I built something and nobody cared." The pain is real and costly — months of wasted dev time, abandoned prototypes, unfocused pitches — but it sits one layer below the surface. The buyer may not be searching for this solution by name. That's not a dealbreaker at this price point, but it means the marketing has to name the pain before selling the fix.

The people who *have* hit this wall feel it acutely. The challenge is reaching them before they've already sunk the time.

---

### 2. Specificity of Audience

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **4 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | Narrow enough to target. Broad enough to sustain. |

"Programmer with a game idea who hasn't shipped before" is a targetable person. They hang out in specific places (r/gamedev, r/IndieDev, game jam communities, Unity/Godot forums, indie dev Twitter/Bluesky). They self-identify. They post about their projects. They consume game design content.

The risk is trying to serve too many sub-audiences at once. A solo hobbyist and a 3-person studio lead have different needs and different sophistication levels. The $19 tier can skew hobbyist; the $39 tier can skew semi-pro. But the messaging needs to pick a primary face. Recommendation: lead with the solo programmer who has a concept and no design training.

---

### 3. Real Value Density

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **4 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | Strong at $19. Needs to clearly justify $39. |

At $19, this is a near-impulse purchase that saves someone 10-40 hours of unstructured flailing. That's a clean value exchange. The product just needs to deliver structured artifacts that are obviously more useful than what someone would produce by staring at a blank Notion doc.

At $39, the buyer expects noticeably more. Either more depth, more artifacts, or a clearer "finished package" at the end. The $39 tier needs to feel like a different product tier, not just the same worksheets with a bonus PDF stapled on.

The core risk: if the worksheets feel like something you could replicate with 20 minutes of ChatGPT prompting, the perceived value collapses. The structure, sequence, and pressure-testing methodology need to be genuinely better than what a naive AI conversation produces.

---

### 4. Trust / Credibility

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **3 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | Credibility is the biggest open question. |

The buyer will ask: "Why should I trust this person's game design framework?" Possible proof sources:

- **Shipped games** — even small ones. Concrete > theoretical.
- **Design background** — professional experience, if it exists.
- **Community presence** — known in indie dev spaces, active, helpful.
- **Artifact quality** — the sales page itself can demonstrate design thinking by showing real examples from the toolkit.
- **Testimonials / beta users** — even 3-5 early users with specific feedback.

If the seller has no visible game design track record, this is the single highest-risk dimension. The product quality can compensate, but only if potential buyers can preview enough of it to judge for themselves before purchase. At $19, the trust bar is low. At $39, it's meaningfully higher.

**What's needed:** At minimum, a public-facing reason to believe this person knows what structured game design looks like. A portfolio piece, a breakdown post, a jam game with a design write-up — something.

---

### 5. Differentiation

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **3 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | The space is noisy. The angle is defensible but must be made explicit. |

What already exists:

| Competitor / Alternative | Why it's different from this product |
|---|---|
| Free GDD templates (Notion, Google Docs) | Downstream artifacts. Don't help with upstream concept clarity. |
| Game design YouTube / courses (GMTK, GDC talks) | Education, not tooling. You learn principles but don't get structured outputs for your specific idea. |
| "Use ChatGPT to design your game" threads | No methodology. No pressure-testing. Generates plausible-sounding text, not validated design structure. |
| One-page design doc templates | Format without methodology. Filling in boxes is not the same as testing assumptions. |
| Machinations (systems design tool) | Different stage. For modeling systems, not for concept-phase clarity. |

The differentiation is real: this product sits *upstream* of most alternatives and combines methodology with artifact output. But that distinction has to be hammered relentlessly in the marketing. If the landing page looks like "another game design template," it's dead on arrival.

### 6. Ease of Purchase

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **5 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | Near-frictionless. |

$19 is below the threshold where most indie devs need to think twice. No subscription. No account creation (if sold via Gumroad/Lemon Squeezy). Instant delivery. No ongoing commitment. The decision cost is: "Is this worth skipping lunch for?" At $39, it's still low-friction — two coffees and a sandwich.

No red flags here. This is one of the strongest dimensions.

---

### 7. Ease of Fulfillment

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **5 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | Highly fulfillable. |

Digital product. PDF/worksheet/template bundle. No ongoing support required. No live components. No user accounts. No software to maintain. Can be built in 1-3 focused weeks. Can be iterated after launch based on feedback.

The only fulfillment risk is quality — making sure the worksheets are genuinely well-designed, not just well-formatted. Ugly worksheets that are methodologically sharp will outperform pretty worksheets that are shallow. But both would be ideal.

---

### 8. Risk of Overpromising

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **3 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | Moderate risk. Must be managed actively. |

Danger zones:

| Overpromise risk | How it manifests |
|---|---|
| "Your game idea will be validated" | The toolkit can structure and pressure-test. It cannot validate market demand. Those are different things. |
| "You'll be ready to prototype" | Depends heavily on the buyer's skill level. A complete beginner won't be prototype-ready after filling out worksheets. |
| "Pitch-ready concept package" | If the output doesn't actually look like something you'd show a collaborator or publisher, this claim backfires. |
| "Works for any game idea" | Some concepts are too vague, too derivative, or too ambitious for worksheets to fix. The tool can surface those problems but not solve them. |

Mitigation: be explicit about what the product does and does not do. Frame it as "clarity and structure," not "validation and success." The positioning doc already handles this well — the audit's job is to flag that the marketing copy must maintain that discipline.

---

### 9. Likelihood of Impulse Purchase

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **3 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | Possible but not guaranteed. Depends entirely on marketing execution. |

The $19 price point supports impulse. But the *category* doesn't. People impulse-buy assets, plugins, and tools they can use immediately in their engine. A "concept clarity toolkit" requires the buyer to (a) recognize they have the problem, (b) believe worksheets can solve it, and (c) trust the seller — all within a few seconds of seeing a post.

What would increase impulse likelihood:
- A strong visual hook (before/after of a messy idea vs. a structured concept pack)
- A specific, relatable pain callout ("You've restarted your game project 3 times")
- Social proof, even minimal
- A clear preview of what the output looks like

Without strong creative, this is a "save the post, think about it, forget about it" product. With strong creative, it can convert from a single reel.

### 10. Potential for Organic Short-Form Marketing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Score** | **4 / 5** |
| **Assessment** | High potential. The content format fits short-form natively. |

Strong content angles:

| Format | Example |
|---|---|
| Before/after | "Here's a game idea described in one vague sentence → here's what it looks like after running through the concept toolkit" |
| Hot take / myth-bust | "Your game design doc is not a design document. It's a wishlist." |
| Pain callout | "You don't have a game design problem. You have a concept clarity problem." |

Gamedev content performs well on short-form. The audience is engaged, opinionated, and shares actively. The product's methodology is inherently demonstrable — you can show the process working in 30-60 seconds. This is a strong organic channel if the creator is willing to post consistently.

### Confirmed

- The price point is within impulse range for this audience
- The fulfillment model is proven (digital product, instant delivery, no ongoing support)
- Free alternatives exist but are unstructured (GDD templates, ChatGPT, YouTube)
- The content marketing channel fits the product natively

### Assumed

- The $39 tier delivers enough additional value to justify the price gap
- The seller has or can build enough credibility to clear the trust threshold

- Self-guided format limits the depth of pressure-testing (no human feedback loop)

- Whether the $19 or $39 tier will be the primary seller

## Strongest Angle

**The upstream positioning.** Almost everything in the indie game design space targets people who are already building. This product targets the moment *before* building — the decision of what to build and whether it's worth building. That's a genuinely underserved moment. If the marketing can make buyers feel the cost of skipping this step (wasted months, abandoned projects, unfocused pitches), the product sells itself at $19.

The combination of "concept clarity + pressure-testing + structured artifacts" is specific, demonstrable, and defensible. It's not a course. It's not a template. It's not coaching. That clarity of category is an asset.

---

## Weakest Angle

**Credibility and the "I could just use ChatGPT" objection.** These are two faces of the same problem. If the buyer doesn't trust the seller's design methodology, and they believe AI can approximate the same output for free, there's no purchase. The product has to demonstrate — visually and methodologically — that its structure produces outputs a naive ChatGPT session cannot. That means the worksheets need to be opinionated, sequenced, and designed to surface non-obvious insights. If they're just "fill in your game's genre, audience, and core mechanic," they lose to a free template.

The credibility gap is solvable but cannot be ignored. The seller needs at least one visible proof point that they understand game design at a structural level. A single well-executed public teardown of a game concept — posted as content — would do more for credibility than any amount of landing page copy.

---

## Verdict: GO

**Rationale:**

The fundamentals are sound. Real pain, targetable audience, clean delivery model, strong price point, natural content marketing fit. None of the weaknesses are structural — they're all execution problems with known solutions.

The two conditions for GO:

1. **The toolkit must be genuinely better than ChatGPT + a blank doc.** This means opinionated frameworks, specific pressure-test questions, non-obvious design filters, and structured artifact outputs. If the product is just "fill in these fields," it fails. If it forces the buyer to confront uncomfortable truths about their concept, it wins.

2. **The seller must establish minimum credibility before launch.** At least 2-3 public content pieces demonstrating the methodology in action. A teardown, a before/after, a process walkthrough. This is non-negotiable for the $39 tier and strongly recommended for the $19 tier.

If both conditions are met, this product is a clean low-ticket offer with strong organic marketing potential and a viable path to a small but real revenue stream.

If either condition is unmet, downgrade to REVISE and address before launch.
