# Positioning

> Specialist: Copy and Positioning Lead + Senior Game Design Director
> Status: DRAFT — pre-validation

---

## Core Positioning Statement

**For programmers and indie creators who have a game idea but no design structure**, this product is a concept clarity toolkit that converts vague inspiration into pressure-tested, structured design artifacts — so they can decide whether to prototype with confidence, not hope.

---

## Alternate Positioning Angles

### Angle 1: The Anti-GDD
> Most game design documents are written too late, too long, and too optimistic. This toolkit forces you to answer the hard questions before you waste months building the wrong thing.

**Best for:** Programmers who've already started and restarted projects. Resonates with sunk-cost pain.

### Angle 2: The Design Pressure Test
> Your game idea is a hypothesis. This toolkit is the test. Run your concept through structured design filters and find out what's strong, what's weak, and what should be cut — before you write a line of code.

**Best for:** Analytical builders who respect methodology. Frames the product as a tool, not a course.

### Angle 3: The Concept-to-Pitch Accelerator
> Go from "I have this idea for a game" to a structured concept pack with defined mechanics, loops, audience, scope, and risks — in a weekend, not a quarter.

**Best for:** Small teams needing alignment artifacts. Emphasizes speed and output.

## Anti-Positioning List

This product is explicitly NOT:

| We are NOT | Why this matters |
|------------|-----------------|
| A game design course | No curriculum, no lessons, no progression. It's a toolkit you use on your own idea. |
| A replacement for production experience | It helps before production. It doesn't teach you how to ship. |
| An AI game builder | No code generation, no asset creation, no "press button get game." |
| A consulting service | No calls, no reviews, no personalized feedback. Self-service only. |
| A GDD template | GDDs are downstream. This product works upstream — at the concept clarity stage. |
| A guaranteed success formula | It helps you make better design decisions. It doesn't promise your game will succeed. |
| An idea generator | You bring the idea. This product tests, structures, and sharpens it. |

## Promise / Proof / Limitation Model

### Promise
After using this toolkit, you will have a structured concept package that clearly defines what your game is, who it's for, what the core loop is, what's risky, and whether it's worth prototyping.

### Proof
- Based on professional game design methodology (core loops, design pillars, mechanic specification, scope filtering)
- Frameworks adapted from shipped game development practice, not theoretical academia
- Structured artifacts that real teams use to align and make decisions
- The toolkit forces concrete outputs — not vague brainstorming

### Limitation
- This product works at the concept phase. It does not help with production, engineering, art, or marketing.
- It requires an existing idea. It does not generate ideas for you.
- It is self-guided. There is no expert review, no coaching, no community.
- It cannot guarantee your concept is good — it can only guarantee you'll understand it clearly.

## Positioning Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|------|----------|------------|
| Confused with generic GDD templates (free everywhere) | HIGH | Emphasize upstream positioning. "Before the GDD." Differentiate on methodology, not format. |
| Perceived as too basic for experienced designers | MEDIUM | Scope the audience clearly. Don't claim it's for senior designers. |
| Perceived as too abstract for programmers who want code | MEDIUM | Show concrete artifacts. Worksheets with fields, not theory paragraphs. |
| "I can just use ChatGPT for this" objection | HIGH | The value is in the structure, sequence, and pressure-testing methodology — not in generating text. Emphasize the framework, not the content. |
| Sounds like a $5 Notion template | MEDIUM | Visual quality of the product and landing page must signal premium craft. |
